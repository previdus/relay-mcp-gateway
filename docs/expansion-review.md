# Five-round research review — 17 October 2026

This is a second audit of the original 20 articles/10 papers plus **50 additional distinct research papers, in five groups of 10**. The linked primary versions were retrieved and selected substantive method/design, evaluation, and scope/discussion sections reviewed. This does not claim every page, proof or appendix was read. No abstract-only source is counted. No external model benchmark was reproduced. Paper findings below are original summaries; decisions are our engineering inferences, not the authors' product recommendations.

Selection is curated for relevance and established research contributions, not a verified worldwide ranking of researchers or a census of the latest papers. The corpus includes author-affiliated work from Microsoft Research (e.g. R1P01/05, R3P03), OpenAI (R1P03), Meta/FAIR (R1P04, R3P01), Google DeepMind (R4P06), Apple (R5P05), NVIDIA (R3P06/09), AI21 (R3P04), Sierra (R2P03/04), Allen Institute for AI (R2P05, R3P06), Hugging Face (R5P07), and university groups. Reputation did not substitute for evaluating methods and limits.

All links pin **v1**. Titles follow that version: SecAlign and Reflexion differ from their later well-known titles. An initial ToolNet identifier resolved to an unrelated astronomy paper; title checking rejected it and the correct 2403.00839 paper replaced it. That unrelated text is not counted. Corrected titles/identifiers and retrieval hashes are recorded in [expansion-sources.json](expansion-sources.json). Historical model/cost figures are not presented as current product facts.

## Round 1: Security boundaries — 10 papers

| Paper | Finding and limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| R1P01 — [Benchmarking and Defending Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks on Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.14197v1) | Boundary identification and utility must be tested separately. BIPIA studies both prompting and training defenses; its simulated, non-multiturn cases cannot establish complete protection. | Implement independent inspectors; defer model training. |
| R1P02 — [StruQ: Defending Against Prompt Injection with Structured Queries](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.06363v1) | StruQ separates instruction and data through formatting plus training. Its own limitations include residual adaptive attacks and a narrower programmatic-application scope. A gateway delimiter alone would not implement StruQ. | Preserve explicit trust boundaries; do not rewrite prompts as an authorization defense. |
| R1P03 — [The Instruction Hierarchy: Training LLMs to Prioritize Privileged Instructions](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13208v1) | The instruction hierarchy is trained through synthetic composition/ignorance examples and evaluated for robustness and over-refusal. The paper acknowledges remaining adversarial and multimodal work. | Keep authorization independent of model obedience; retain benign-use tests. |
| R1P04 — [Aligning LLMs to Be Robust Against Prompt Injection (SecAlign, v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05451v1) | SecAlign uses preference optimization toward desirable and away from injected responses. The v1 title is Aligning LLMs to Be Robust Against Prompt Injection. It requires clear input separation and does not claim complete security. | Defer training; expose a veto-only adapter boundary for deployment-owned defenses. |
| R1P05 — [Defending Against Indirect Prompt Injection Attacks With Spotlighting](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14720v1) | Spotlighting marks untrusted content through delimiters, datamarking or encoding. Its keyword attack evaluation and older models limit generalization; encoding can damage utility. | Reject automatic result re-encoding; provide explicit result-inspection integration. |
| R1P06 — [AgentHarm: A Benchmark for Measuring Harmfulness of LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09024v1) | AgentHarm measures harmful multi-step compliance with synthetic tools, rather than general safety. English prompts and constrained initial user attacks limit coverage. | Retain concrete effect checks and separate success from denial outcomes. |
| R1P07 — [Agent-SafetyBench: Evaluating the Safety of LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14470v1) | Agent-SafetyBench covers varied risk categories and uses a calibrated learned scorer. Its own scorer construction shows that a generic model judge is imperfect. | No classifier verdict grants access; exceptions/timeouts deny. |
| R1P08 — [Agent Security Bench (ASB): Formalizing and Benchmarking Attacks and Defenses in LLM-based Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02644v1) | ASB formalizes prompt, observation, memory and backdoor attack surfaces, with several defense families. Versions differ in benchmark counts; results depend on chosen agents/tools. | Add a result boundary and immutable per-call contexts; do not invent workflow provenance. |
| R1P09 — [AgentPoison: Red-teaming LLM Agents via Poisoning Memory or Knowledge Bases](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12784v1) | AgentPoison attacks retrieval memories through optimized triggers and reports retrieval/action/environment outcomes separately. Its attacker has embedder access, with transfer studied empirically. | Keep shared model memory out of the gateway; test actual upstream effects. |
| R1P10 — [Jailbreaking Leading Safety-Aligned LLMs with Simple Adaptive Attacks](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.02151v1) | Adaptive jailbreaks show that a fixed prompt defense needs evaluation against an attacker that responds to it. Access assumptions and model versions matter. | Use deterministic authorization as the floor; retain pluggable inspectors without immunity claims. |

## Round 2: Stateful evaluation — 10 papers

| Paper | Finding and limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| R2P01 — [ToolLLM: Facilitating Large Language Models to Master 16000+ Real-world APIs](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.16789v1) | ToolLLM builds tool-use trajectories and retrieval over a large API inventory. External API availability and automatic grading affect reproducibility. | Bound discovery and avoid mistaking generated trajectories for real integration evidence. |
| R2P02 — [API-Bank: A Benchmark for Tool-Augmented LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.08244v1) | API-Bank uses executable APIs, databases and tool management to separate searching, planning and calling. Its finite API/task inventory limits generalization. | Use state assertions and reuse one authorized catalog for search and calls. |
| R2P03 — [tau-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12045v1) | tau-bench evaluates policy-following interactions and database outcomes with a simulated user. Repeated-run reliability and simulator ambiguity matter. | Test denied and permitted state changes; do not claim model reliability from one gateway test. |
| R2P04 — [tau2-bench: Evaluating Conversational Agents in a Dual-Control Environment](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07982v1) | tau2-bench adds user-controlled state and studies simulator/task/implementation errors separately. It does not fully model expert-novice communication gaps. | Add concurrent-call isolation and pending-request enforcement tests. |
| R2P05 — [AppWorld: A Controllable World of Apps and People for Benchmarking Interactive Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.18901v1) | AppWorld evaluates executable cross-app tasks through process-independent checks. API coverage excludes some UI-only applications, and generated task environments need validation. | Check resulting tool state rather than forcing one transcript or planner. |
| R2P06 — [OSWorld: Benchmarking Multimodal Agents for Open-Ended Tasks in Real Computer Environments](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.07972v1) | OSWorld uses task-specific execution evaluators in real computer environments. Grounding, evaluation functions and software environments impose distinct limitations. | Retain real Chromium flows; do not call HTTP tests full computer-agent evaluation. |
| R2P07 — [WebArena: A Realistic Web Environment for Building Autonomous Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.13854v1) | WebArena provides self-hosted websites and functional task evaluation. Environment state and task coverage differ from arbitrary live websites. | Prefer isolated repeatable integration environments with explicit initial state. |
| R2P08 — [VisualWebArena: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Realistic Visual Web Tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13649v1) | VisualWebArena adds visually grounded tasks and evaluation primitives. Exact and semantic graders cover different output types, with benchmark-specific visual scope. | Keep browser interaction checks; defer a visual-agent benchmark that the gateway does not implement. |
| R2P09 — [SWE-bench: Can Language Models Resolve Real-World GitHub Issues?](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06770v1) | SWE-bench evaluates patches against execution tests and repository issues. The original v1 scope is Python, and tests alone do not guarantee all quality properties. | Retain regression suites plus manual boundary review; do not advertise a SWE-bench score. |
| R2P10 — [StableToolBench: Towards Stable Large-Scale Benchmarking on Tool Learning of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07714v1) | StableToolBench uses cached/simulated APIs and revised grading to reduce external API instability. Simulation can stabilize testing while diverging from actual services. | Use real PostgreSQL/OpenFGA and HTTP fixtures for release gates, not simulated success. |

## Round 3: Tool orchestration — 10 papers

| Paper | Finding and limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| R3P01 — [Toolformer: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Use Tools](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04761v1) | Toolformer learns when and how to call tools through self-supervised data. The original method explicitly limits chaining and interactive search. | No new gateway training loop; keep tool contracts and live schema validation. |
| R3P02 — [Gorilla: Large Language Model Connected with Massive APIs](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15334v1) | Gorilla combines API documentation retrieval with model training and constraint-aware API selection. Its original APIBench emphasizes ML APIs. | Recheck definitions and offer bounded authorized discovery; no assumed universal API accuracy. |
| R3P03 — [HuggingGPT: Solving AI Tasks with ChatGPT and its Friends in Hugging Face](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17580v1) | HuggingGPT separates planning, model selection, execution and response generation. It acknowledges multiple-inference latency, context and stability limits. | Keep orchestration outside the gateway and expose a small typed integration interface. |
| R3P04 — [MRKL Systems: A modular neuro-symbolic architecture that combines large language models, external knowledge sources and discrete reasoning](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.00445v1) | MRKL advocates distinct neural and symbolic modules with explicit routing/parameter transfer. Its illustrative arithmetic evaluation is narrower than general agent execution. | Maintain separate routing, authorization and inspection modules; do not let routing confer authority. |
| R3P05 — [Reflexion: an autonomous agent with dynamic memory and self-reflection (v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.11366v1) | The original Reflexion v1 uses heuristic feedback and episodic self-reflection. Its title/authorship differ from later revisions; grading feedback can be imperfect. | Keep self-reflection outside policy authority; use independently checked state outcomes. |
| R3P06 — [Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17651v1) | Self-Refine iterates generation, feedback and refinement with the same model across task-specific prompts. Improvement is task-dependent and feedback is not an independent verifier. | No self-approval of privilege changes; retain deterministic checks. |
| R3P07 — [An LLM Compiler for Parallel Function Calling](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.04511v1) | LLMCompiler separates task planning, dependency dispatch and parallel execution. Benchmark latency gains depend on dependency structure, model and tools. | Bound discovery parallelism; do not parallelize or retry arbitrary side effects automatically. |
| R3P08 — [ReWOO: Decoupling Reasoning from Observations for Efficient Augmented Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18323v1) | ReWOO decouples foreseeable reasoning from observations. It explicitly struggles where the environment requires reactive exploration and may need replanning. | Keep requests independent and cancellable; no static planner embedded in authorization. |
| R3P09 — [Voyager: An Open-Ended Embodied Agent with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291v1) | Voyager uses curriculum, a skill library and iterative execution feedback in Minecraft. It documents cost, hallucination and verification failures. | Do not run self-generated skills inside the gateway process; preserve a separate sandbox requirement. |
| R3P10 — [Plan-and-Solve Prompting: Improving Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Reasoning by Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.04091v1) | Plan-and-Solve decomposes reasoning and studies arithmetic/common-sense tasks. Prompt sensitivity and semantic misunderstandings remain. | Use an explicit implementation/test plan, not a planning prompt as a security guarantee. |

| Paper | Finding and limitation | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| R4P01 — [Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v1) | Generative Agents combines memory retrieval, reflection and planning for believable social simulations. Believability and emergent behavior do not demonstrate permission correctness. | Defer agent memory; avoid cross-tenant payload retention in the gateway. |
| R4P02 — [MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08560v1) | MemGPT manages virtual context using memory tiers and paginated retrieval. | R4P03 — [Augmenting Language Models with Long-Term Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.07174v1) | LongMem adds a trained SideNet and cached representations to a frozen model. Its long-text/NLU results require model-level machinery. | Defer model changes; keep metadata delivery bounded and credentials outside context. |
| R4P04 — [MemoryBank: Enhancing Large Language Models with Long-Term Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10250v1) | MemoryBank stores/retrieves/updates memories and user summaries for extended interaction. | R4P05 — [Reasoning with Language Model is Planning with World Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14992v1) | RAP uses a language-model world model and tree search over possible outcomes. Predicted state and confidence are not actual external state or authorization. | Do not let simulated outcomes authorize execution or count as integration evidence. |
| R4P06 — [Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.10601v1) | Tree of Thoughts explores alternative reasoning paths with search and evaluation. | R4P07 — [Graph of Thoughts: Solving Elaborate Problems with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09687v1) | Graph of Thoughts organizes aggregation and refinement as graph operations with cost-aware comparisons. | R4P08 — [Language Agent Tree Search Unifies Reasoning Acting and Planning in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.04406v1) | LATS combines tree search, reflections and interaction across reasoning/programming/WebShop tasks. | R4P09 — [Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03172v1) | Lost in the Middle measures retrieval/QA sensitivity to where evidence occurs in context. | R4P10 — [Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14627v1) | ALCE separates correctness and citation support, and explores retrieval/summarization strategies. |---|---|---|
| R5P01 — [ToolAlpaca: Generalized Tool Learning for Language Models with 3000 Simulated Cases](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05301v1) | ToolAlpaca generates diverse simulated tool-use data for generalization. Generated interactions and fine-tuning results do not prove real API behavior. | Defer training data generation; retain live schema and integration checks. |
| R5P02 — [ToolkenGPT: Augmenting Frozen Language Models with Massive Tools via Tool Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.11554v1) | ToolkenGPT learns tool embeddings with a frozen model instead of placing all descriptions in context. | R5P03 — [ART: Automatic multi-step reasoning and tool-use for large language models](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.09014v1) | ART interleaves generated programs and tool calls using task/tool libraries. Human edits help demonstrations, but code execution requires a separate trust boundary. | Keep code generation outside the gateway; mediate every eventual tool invocation. |
| R5P04 — [AnyTool: Self-Reflective Hierarchical Agents for Large-Scale API Calls](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04253v1) | AnyTool uses hierarchical API discovery and self-reflection, and revises evaluation of candidate APIs. Its solvability selection and model judge affect reported pass rates. | Implement authorized lexical search as a bounded baseline; defer hierarchical/learned ranking pending measurements. |
| R5P05 — [ToolSandbox: A Stateful, Conversational, Interactive Evaluation Benchmark for LLM Tool Use Capabilities](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.04682v1) | ToolSandbox evaluates intermediate milestones and forbidden minefields in stateful conversations. Authoring and user-simulator errors remain; mandatory confirmation/authentication are outside its current scope. | Test permitted progress and forbidden effects, including withheld results; do not imply human approval is solved. |
| R5P06 — [AgentBench: Evaluating LLMs as Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03688v1) | AgentBench covers multiple interactive environments with executable checking pipelines. Aggregation across domains and sampled environments is not universal agent competence. | Keep scenario-level evidence and isolate fixtures; no aggregate model score claimed. |
| R5P07 — [GAIA: a benchmark for General AI Assistants](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.12983v1) | GAIA evaluates assistants on practical questions and tool-dependent skills. Answer grading, tool selection and static task coverage limit interpretation. | Expose useful tool discovery; do not equate discoverability with autonomous task completion. |
| R5P08 — [Mind2Web: Towards a Generalist Agent for the Web](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.06070v1) | Mind2Web uses candidate generation before LLM action selection because raw HTML is too large. Website generalization and offline action matching differ from live execution. | Use authorization before catalog ranking, preserving call-time checks after selection. |
| R5P09 — [WebShop: Towards Scalable Real-World Web Interaction with Grounded Language Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.01206v1) | WebShop separates website transitions, task instructions and rewards for grounded interaction. Transfer from simulation to real purchasing remains a safety boundary. | Do not retry/commit effects based on a simulated reward; retain explicit argument limits. |
| R5P10 — [ToolNet: Connecting Large Language Models with Massive Tools via Tool Graph](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00839v1) | ToolNet organizes tools as a graph with learned transition weights. It needs multi-hop trajectories and evaluates selected subsets with one model in v1. | Implement bounded search first; defer graph learning until private data, utility and authorization-preserving ranking are evaluated. |

## Iteration outcomes and limits

Round 1 produced the inspection extension and original-pin fix; round 2 added stateful/concurrent/failure/pending-result regressions; round 3 consolidated a typed versioned plugin entry point and kept planners outside the trusted data plane; round 4 produced bounded collection and standard stateless pagination; round 5 adds authorized search using the same collector. Validation status and exact counts belong in validation.md, not in paper benchmark tables. No round requires inventing a gateway feature for every paper: model training, memory stores and planner search are deliberately deferred where they would increase coupling, retention or trusted execution scope.

This review supports specific design changes and documented tradeoffs. It does not establish exhaustive literature coverage, absence of bugs, universal prompt-injection defense or that competitors lack equivalent features. The subsequent 10-gateway review uses separate primary-source evidence and edition labels.

Final acceptance: 80 tests passed with no failures/skips, plus hardened container and two-replica Kubernetes smoke checks, outage recovery and actual prior-image rollback/promotion. See [validation.md](validation.md) for evidence and boundaries.
